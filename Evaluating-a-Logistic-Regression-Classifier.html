5.3 Accuracy

The simplest score: what fraction did we get right?

Accuracy = (TP + TN) / Total

Easy to understand, but dangerous with imbalanced data. If only 1% of emails are spam, a model that always predicts "not spam" is 99% accurate — and completely useless.

5.4 Precision & Recall

Two questions accuracy can't answer alone.

Precision

Of everything I flagged "Yes," how much was right?

TP / (TP + FP)

High precision = few false alarms.

Recall

Of everything that was actually "Yes," how much did I catch?

TP / (TP + FN)

High recall = few misses.

They usually trade off — catching more "Yes" cases (recall) often means more false alarms (lower precision).

5.5 F1 Score

One number that balances precision and recall.

F1 = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)

F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall — it stays low unless both are reasonably high, making it a good single score when you care about both false alarms and misses.

5.6 ROC Curve & AUC

How good is the model across every possible threshold?

Instead of picking one threshold, the ROC curve plots the trade-off between catching true positives and triggering false alarms across all thresholds at once. AUC (Area Under the Curve) summarizes it in one number — 1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is random guessing.
